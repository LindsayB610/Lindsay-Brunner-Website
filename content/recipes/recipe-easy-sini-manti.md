I had no idea what sini manti were until I saw a version on Instagram. Then I did the useful thing and tracked it back to the [Serious Eats recipe](https://www.seriouseats.com/sini-manti-armenian-baked-manti) that actually explains the dish. Now I’m obsessed. Spiced beef in tiny wonton-wrapper canoes sounds like a silly amount of fuss until you realize each wrapper gets cut into four and no one is rolling a hundred dumplings.

I made a few small tweaks to the sauce, but this is exactly the kind of dinner I’m thrilled to put into regular rotation: crisp little manti, something cool and garlicky, something red and saucy, and a sheet pan of whichever vegetables need using up. Weeknight dinner with enough going on to feel like a plan.

## Snapshot

- **Implements:** large mixing bowl; box grater or knife; cutting board; knife or kitchen shears; baking dish
- **Oven setting:** 375°F (190°C) on convection for about 30 minutes
- **Batch size:** 1 baking dish (servings not specified in the source)
- **Notes:** The source’s caption says only “375,” with no temperature unit. This draft interprets that as Fahrenheit.

## Ingredients

### Beef manti

- 1 lb ground beef (the source uses 85/15)
- 1 onion, finely grated or minced, with excess liquid squeezed out
- 1½ Tsp kosher salt
- ½ Tsp black pepper
- 1 Tsp Aleppo pepper
- A handful fresh parsley, chopped
- 1 package wonton wrappers
- Olive oil, for the baking dish and top

### Roasted red pepper tomato sauce

- 1 Tbsp olive oil
- 1 Tbsp tomato paste
- 1 Tsp pepper paste
- 1 jar roasted red peppers, drained and blended smooth (jar size unverified)
- 1 Tsp salt
- Up to 1 cup water or chicken stock, as needed to loosen the sauce

### Garlicky yogurt

- 1 cup yogurt
- 2 cloves garlic, crushed
- 1 Tsp salt

## Method

1. **Heat the oven.**

   Heat the oven to 375°F (190°C) on convection. Oil a baking dish generously enough that the manti will not weld themselves to it in protest.

2. **Make the beef filling.**

   Grate or finely mince the onion, then squeeze out the excess liquid. Add it to a large bowl with the beef, kosher salt, black pepper, Aleppo pepper, and parsley. Mix until evenly combined.

3. **Cut, fill, and pinch the wontons.**

   Cut each wonton wrapper into four equal squares. Put a small amount of beef filling in the center of each one. Pinch the two opposite ends together to form a small canoe, leaving the center open. Arrange the manti in the oiled baking dish as you go.

4. **Oil and bake.**

   Drizzle the tops with a little more olive oil. Bake for about 30 minutes, until the manti are slightly crisp and golden.

5. **Make the roasted red pepper tomato sauce.**

   In a small saucepan over low heat, combine the olive oil, tomato paste, and pepper paste. Stir in the blended roasted red peppers, then whisk in water or chicken stock as needed until the sauce is smooth and spoonable. Season with salt and set aside while the manti finishes baking.

6. **Make the garlic yogurt.**

   Mix the yogurt, crushed garlic, and salt. Taste it. It should be assertively garlicky and salty enough to wake up the beef.

7. **Serve.**

   Serve the hot manti with the garlic yogurt and roasted red pepper tomato sauce.

## Notes, swaps, and guardrails

### Roasted red pepper sauce: test the ratio

The source's tomato paste and pepper paste sauce is now being adapted with blended jarred roasted red peppers. Mint is intentionally gone.
